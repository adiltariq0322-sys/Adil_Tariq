# ChatGPT Ads just went global — but the early numbers say test before you spend

Two weeks ago, ChatGPT became a storefront. On November 10, OpenAI let businesses claim a verified profile inside ChatGPT and, for eligible US sellers, publish an AI agent that answers shoppers' questions. On November 25, the next step arrived: an advertiser update that opens Business Agents to eligible businesses outside the United States and adds the measurement tools a serious ad channel needs. The same week, the first numbers on whether ChatGPT Ads pay arrived — and they are sobering.

## What changed on November 25

**Business Agents are going international.** At launch, only eligible US e-commerce and online service businesses could publish an agent that chats with shoppers inside ChatGPT. The update extends that to eligible businesses in supported markets outside the US. One catch: OpenAI has not published a list of those markets, so whether your country is included is not yet confirmable. You still have to claim your business in Business Hub and pass OpenAI's verification review. Shopify merchants can connect their store directly to speed up verification.

**Ads finally get audience measurement.** Until now, Ads Manager only reported basic numbers like impressions, clicks and spend. The update adds nine audience metrics: Reach, Frequency, Click Reach, Click Frequency, Total Reach, Total Frequency, Unique CTR, Unique CPC and Cost per 1,000 Reached. Reach counts the unique people who saw your ad; Frequency is total impressions divided by Reach. In plain terms: you can now tell whether your ad reaches new people or repeats to the same ones, and Unique CPC tells you what a new visitor costs.

**Smaller changes, one deadline.** Single-image ads now show a "Learn more" link by default, product-feed advertisers can hide sale prices when a promotion ends, and rejected ads now explain which policy was involved. One deadline: from December 9, connections to approved third-party partner apps will be allowed by default — nothing connects automatically, but advertisers have until December 8 to opt out by replying to OpenAI's email.

## The numbers that should slow you down

The expansion looks like a green light. The early return data looks like a yellow one. An Adthena study of 30 enterprise brands (July–September 2026 results, relayed by Search Engine Journal) found 52% struggling just to break even on ChatGPT Ads. Only 26% matched or beat Google Ads; 22% were mixed. A working paper by Kaiser and Schulze (October 2026, not yet peer-reviewed) found US ChatGPT ad traffic returned $0.89 in revenue per visit, against $2.45 for paid search.

Early and partial — one study, one working paper, both about large advertisers — but they match the channel's design. A ChatGPT ad is a sponsored card below the answer, not inside it, so it is easy to ignore. Ads only reach free-plan users. And there are no keywords: you describe what you sell and ChatGPT matches the ad to conversations, so targeting is looser than search, where the person has already typed exactly what they want.

Neil Patel, on November 26, advised keeping ChatGPT and Copilot in a small test budget that has to earn more spend, with Google as the starting point when people already search for what you sell. His key point: there are two ways to win inside ChatGPT — buy the sponsored spot below the answer, or get recommended inside the answer itself. The second is bigger, and it cannot be bought.

## What this means for your business

**Start with the free part.** Claiming your Business Hub profile costs nothing, and everything else rests on it. It gives you control of the facts ChatGPT shows about your business and shows how often ChatGPT mentions you and sends people to your site. OpenAI says ads do not influence its answers; the information about your business online does. Keep your facts consistent everywhere AI looks: your website, your Google Business Profile, your listings. One name, one location, one set of prices and hours.

**If you advertise, treat it as a test.** Put a small, fixed budget on it — money you can afford to learn with — and judge it on Unique CPC versus what a visit is worth to you, not on clicks. Watch Reach and Frequency for fatigue: if Frequency climbs while clicks flatten, the same people are seeing the same ad — change the creative. And a Business Agent only starts the conversation; the sale still closes on your website, because the agent cannot take payments or bookings.

**Fix the website first.** Only businesses with a website can claim a profile, and the agent is only as good as the information behind it. An out-of-date site or wrong prices do not just lose the sale — they teach ChatGPT the wrong facts about you.

The channel is real and growing fast. But new channels reward businesses that test carefully, not ones that rush in. A small, honestly measured test now puts you ahead of competitors who ignore the channel or pour money into it blind.
