# Two-thirds of small businesses now use AI — the question is no longer if, but how well

A week ago, payroll and HR company Gusto released its 2026 State of Small Business report, and one number in it should get every small-business owner's attention: 67% of small businesses now use AI tools for at least some tasks. The survey covered 1,731 owners of U.S. businesses with 24 or fewer employees, polled between August 17 and September 7, 2026.

That is a big shift from just a year ago. AI has moved from something owners were curious about to something they use every week. As Gusto's principal economist, Nich Tremper, put it: "AI is really deepening into the business."

## What the numbers actually say

The headline figure hides an important detail — most businesses are still only scratching the surface. Here is the breakdown:

- **19% are experimenting** — trying AI tools out, but not relying on them yet.
- **28% use AI for some tasks** — it helps here and there, but it is not part of how the business runs.
- **15% use it regularly across multiple functions** — AI is woven into several parts of the operation.
- **5% say AI is built into every part of the business** where the technology allows.

Add those up and you get the 67%. But only 1 in 5 businesses — the last two groups — use AI deeply. The rest are dabbling.

There is a second gap in the report that matters just as much. Only 25% of businesses with employees have a formal AI policy — rules about what AI may and may not do, and who is responsible when it gets something wrong. That is up from 15% last year, but it still means three out of four employers are letting staff use AI with no guardrails at all.

The report also found that 52% of owners raised prices in the past year, and 61% say they feel optimistic about their own business, even while they worry about the wider economy. Owners are confident about what they control — and AI is increasingly one of the things they can control.

## What this means for your business

The trend is real, but the advantage does not go to the businesses that merely "use AI." It goes to the ones that use it properly — on real work, with real rules.

**Start with the work that repeats.** The businesses getting value from AI are not using it to write the occasional social media post. They are applying it to the jobs that happen every day: answering the same customer questions, taking bookings, following up with leads, chasing unpaid invoices, and sorting enquiries. If a task happens ten times a day and follows a pattern, it is a candidate for AI.

**Depth beats dabbling.** A chatbot that only answers a few questions on your website is experimenting. A system that answers questions from your real prices and policies, takes a booking, updates your calendar, and hands over to a human when it should — that is AI doing real work. The 20% using AI across multiple functions are building exactly that kind of system, and they are the ones pulling ahead.

**Write the policy before you need it.** If your staff are already using AI tools — and the numbers say they almost certainly are — a simple policy costs nothing and prevents real problems: which tools are approved, what customer data may never be pasted into them, and who checks AI-written replies before they go out. The report shows most businesses have not done this yet. Doing it now puts you ahead of three-quarters of your competitors.

**Your customers' AI is already talking to you.** This is the part many owners miss. Customers now ask their own AI assistants to find businesses, compare options, and even make calls. If your public information — hours, prices, services — is out of date, the AI reports the wrong facts about you, and the customer books elsewhere without ever speaking to you. Keeping your website and listings accurate is no longer just good marketing; it is how you stay visible to the AI tools your customers use.
