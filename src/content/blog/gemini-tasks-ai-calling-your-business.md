# Google's AI is now phoning your business — is your front desk ready?

On November 17, 2026, Google made a quiet change with loud consequences for small businesses. Gemini, its AI assistant, can now pick up the phone for its users. Flip one toggle in the Gemini app from Chat to Task, describe an errand in plain words, and Gemini will call a business, wait on hold, ask for an appointment, check the customer's calendar, and add the booking itself. It is free, live now, and rolling out to hundreds of millions of people in the US, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia, and India, on Google's new Gemini 4 Hydrogen model.

## What actually happened

Gemini Tasks turns the chatbot into an assistant that finishes jobs instead of just answering questions. Google's example comes from Josh Woodward, the vice president who leads the Gemini app: his minivan was overdue for a service, so he asked Gemini to call the two nearest dealerships and find an early Friday morning appointment. Gemini called both at once, waited on hold, compared the times, checked his calendar, and booked it.

The same feature can also complete purchases through Google Pay — but the phone calls are the part that changes life for a small business. Calls start in the US, Gemini identifies itself as an AI, and businesses can opt out. (Calls are not available in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana, and Nebraska, and Google has given no date for other regions.)

## Your next caller may not be a person

A customer no longer has to remember to call your clinic, your garage, or your restaurant. They tell their phone what they want, and an AI rings you. It will ask about your opening hours, your prices, and your next free slot. It will not get impatient on hold, it will not call back later, and it will not leave a voicemail. It will book with the business that answers clearly — or move on to the next one. The person answering your phone may now be talking to an AI that is comparing you with your competitors in real time, and reporting back to a customer who never heard your voice at all.

Two things decide whether that call becomes a booking. First, can the caller reach a clear answer at all — is someone, or something, answering the phone when they call? Second, is the information accurate: your real hours, your real prices, your real availability? An AI caller does not fill in gaps. If booking only works when a human picks up and knows the diary by heart, the call fails — and the customer books elsewhere without you ever knowing.

## What this means for your business

The steps are small — and they are the same ones that matter for human callers too.

**Answer every call, at every hour.** Gemini calls when the customer asks it to, not when your receptionist is at the desk. Missed calls were always lost bookings; now they are lost bookings you never even see, because the AI moves on to the next business on the list.

**Keep your public information accurate.** Gemini finds businesses through Google Maps and your public listings. Wrong hours, an old phone number, or a missing booking link means the AI either cannot reach you or reports the wrong facts about you. Fix the source — your website, your Google Business Profile, your prices — and every AI that reads it gets the right answer.

**Make booking possible without a human in the loop.** A caller that can check a calendar and confirm a slot needs a booking system it can actually use — a clear booking page, a WhatsApp number that replies instantly, or an AI receptionist that takes the booking itself.

**Do not panic about the opt-out.** Businesses can opt out of Gemini's calls. But opting out does not stop the trend — it only means the customers who use AI assistants will book with your competitors instead.
