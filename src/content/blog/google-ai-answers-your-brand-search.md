# Google's AI now answers for your business before you do

A customer has heard your name — from a friend, a sign, an ad. They type it into Google. For years, the first thing they saw was your website. That is changing: today, the first thing they often read is a short paragraph written by Google's AI, assembled from several sites, with your own site below. You cannot edit that paragraph — only influence what it is built from.

## How common is this now?

Three separate studies, all published in October 2026, measured the same thing and landed in the same place:

- **Ahrefs** analysed 232.8 million US desktop search results from July to September 2026. On September 29, an AI Overview appeared on **82.91% of branded-keyword results** — searches for a business by name — up from a 61.3% monthly average in July. In a 100-brand panel, 48 of 63 AI Overviews sat in position one, above the brand's own site.
- **SE Ranking** checked 1,000 US brand keywords in October 2026 and found AI Overviews on **82.5%** of them. Small and mid-sized business names were slightly *more* likely to get one than big enterprises (78.7% vs 70.7%).
- **DemandSphere** tracked the rise through September: 26.12% on September 1 to 90.48% on September 27.

And it is still moving: since late October, AI Overviews have started appearing in the right-hand column of desktop results, where the knowledge panel used to sit — first spotted by SEO consultant David McSweeney on October 31. Google has not announced the change.

These are industry measurements, not official Google data, and mostly US desktop results weighted towards large brands — so the exact rate for a small business in Lahore or Dubai may be lower. But the direction is the same everywhere AI Overviews are live.

## Who is actually writing your first impression?

An AI Overview does not only read your website. **Slate Research** searched 100 brand names and found that **71% of the sources Google cited were pages the brand did not own** — Wikipedia, YouTube, LinkedIn, review sites, directories, old news stories.

It also does not read your homepage first. SE Ranking found homepages made up 71.1% of brand results in the organic top 10 but only 36.8% of AI Overview citations. The AI reached deeper: product and service pages made up 22.9% of citations, About pages 11.7%. And 62% of the brand pages it quoted did not rank in the top 10 at all. In plain terms, the pages Google's AI reads are often not the pages you think of as your "front door".

This matters because a branded search is your highest-intent visitor — someone checking you out before they call, book or buy. If the summary they read first comes from an outdated listing or a two-year-old video, that is the impression they carry into the decision. Ahrefs found the top-ranking page gets a 58% lower click-through rate when an AI Overview is present: people read the summary and stop there.

## A 30-minute check you can run today

No tool or budget needed. Open a private browser window, search your business name, and do four things:

1. **Read the AI Overview carefully.** Is every fact right — services, location, hours, prices? Is anything out of date or wrong?
2. **Click every source it cites.** Note which ones you own and which you do not. The ones you do not own are shaping your story.
3. **Fix what you control.** Update your Google Business Profile first — one of the most cited sources — then your own website: clear service pages, an accurate About page, real prices and hours, and a FAQ answering the questions customers actually ask.
4. **Make the rest consistent.** Directories, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn — same name, address and hours everywhere. The AI builds its answer from all of it, and contradictions become its mistakes.

Repeat the check monthly. AI Overviews change often; one check is a snapshot, not a verdict.

## What this means for your business

The old rule was simple: rank first for your own name and you owned your first impression. That rule is gone. The paragraph above the rankings now matters more, and it is written from everything the web says about you.

The businesses that win are the ones whose own pages are so clear, complete and consistent that the AI has good material to work with. That means service pages the AI can quote, a FAQ in plain language, and a chatbot on your own site that answers from your own documents instead of leaving the question to Google.
