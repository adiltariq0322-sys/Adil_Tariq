# ChatGPT just became a storefront — what your business should do about it

On November 10, 2026, OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta. For the first time, a business can claim a verified profile inside ChatGPT, correct the details the chatbot shows about it, add a product feed, and see how often ChatGPT mentions it and sends people to its website. Eligible US e-commerce and online service businesses can go further and publish their own AI agent that talks to shoppers inside ChatGPT.

## What actually launched

The launch has two main pieces, both run through a dashboard called Business Hub, at chatgpt.com/business-hub.

**Verified business profiles.** An eligible business can claim its listing in ChatGPT, verify it, and take control of what the chatbot shows: its website, description, logo, and delivery and return policies. Retailers can also add a product feed with prices and availability, so ChatGPT's answers about what you sell can be current instead of guessed from old web pages.

**Business Agents.** After claiming a profile, eligible US e-commerce and online service businesses can build, test, and launch their own AI agent inside ChatGPT. The agent chats with shoppers on the business's behalf, in a conversation that is clearly labelled as AI and kept separate from the shopper's main ChatGPT thread.

**Insights.** Business Hub reports, over the previous 28 days, how often ChatGPT mentions a business, how many people click through to its website, and which topics bring people in. Businesses do not see individual users' conversations.

## The limits that decide whether it helps you

First, the sale still closes on your site. The agent cannot take payments, make bookings, or look up an order. It can answer questions and guide a shopper; the transaction happens where it always did.

Second, Business Agents are US-only for now. Only eligible US e-commerce and online service businesses can publish one, and OpenAI has announced no fixed timeline for expanding to other countries or industries.

Third, claiming a profile does not guarantee higher placement or more sales — OpenAI says this plainly. What it gives you is control of your facts. Fourth, at launch, only businesses with a website can claim a profile, so an out-of-date or missing site closes this door until it is fixed.

## What this means for your business

Think of Business Hub as the Google Business Profile of the AI-answer world — except it lives inside ChatGPT. The same logic that made a Google listing essential now applies here, because that is where a growing number of customers ask their questions first. OpenAI says 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT every week; when one of them asks for a recommendation, ChatGPT answers from what it knows. Until this month, businesses had no official way to manage what it said about them. Now they do.

The practical steps are small, and most of them are free. Claiming a profile and publishing an agent cost nothing at launch. The work is getting your facts straight: a current website, a clear description of what you sell, correct prices, and honest delivery and return policies. If ChatGPT's answers about your business are wrong today, it is because your public information is thin, outdated, or scattered — and that is fixable.

For a business outside the US, the agent itself is not available yet, but the profile is. Claiming it now means that when a customer asks ChatGPT about your kind of business, the information it uses is yours, not something pieced together from a stale listing. And when the agent feature expands, the businesses that already have a claimed profile and a clean product feed will be ready on day one.

There is a second lesson here. ChatGPT is now a channel where customers discover, compare, and ask — and the answers come from your public information. A product feed with accurate prices and stock is no longer just a Google Shopping concern; it is what an AI agent reads before it recommends you.
